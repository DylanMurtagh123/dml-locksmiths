What to Do If You're Locked Out of Your Home — A Step-by-Step Guide

Sean, DML Locksmiths
26 April 2026
~6 min read
Ipswich, Suffolk

Getting locked out of your home is one of those situations that can instantly turn a normal day into a stressful one. Whether it's late at night, pouring with rain, or you're in a rush to get somewhere, standing outside your own front door without a key is never ideal. The good news? Before you panic, there are practical steps you can take to handle it calmly and safely. In this guide, Sean from DML Locksmiths walks you through exactly what to do — and what to avoid.

1

Stay Calm and Assess the Situation

First things first — don't panic. It's easy to feel overwhelmed or frustrated, especially if you're tired, cold, or in a hurry. But rushing into decisions can lead to mistakes and sometimes costly damage.

Take a moment and ask yourself:

  • Have I definitely lost my key, or could it be nearby?
  • Is there another way into the property?
  • Do I have a spare key stored somewhere or with someone?

A clear head will help you think through your options properly before taking action.

2

Check All Doors and Windows

It might sound obvious, but it's worth checking thoroughly. Walk around your property and look for back doors, side entrances, ground floor windows and garage doors. Sometimes a window may have been left slightly open, or a secondary door may not be fully locked.

Important

Only attempt entry through your own property and avoid anything that could cause damage or put you at risk. If something requires force — stop.

3

Think About Spare Keys

Many people forget they've planned for this exact situation. Think carefully — do you have a spare key with a trusted neighbour, a family member who has a copy, or a key stored somewhere safe? If you live with others, give them a call to see if they can let you in.

4

Avoid These Common Mistakes

This is where things can go wrong. When people are locked out, they often try quick fixes that end up making the situation worse or more expensive.

Don't try to force the door

Kicking or pushing the door can damage the lock, crack the door frame and lead to expensive repairs.

Don't attempt DIY lock picking

Unless you're trained, this is unlikely to work — and can damage the internal mechanism, making a locksmith's job harder and more expensive.

Don't break a window (unless there's a genuine emergency)

Breaking glass should only be an absolute last resort if someone is in danger inside. It creates safety hazards, higher costs, and more stress.

Be cautious of "quick hacks" online

Credit card tricks and similar internet methods rarely work on modern locks — and often cause damage that makes entry even harder.

5

Consider the Time and Your Safety

Your next step can depend on your circumstances. Ask yourself: is it daytime or late at night? Are you in a safe area? Is the weather putting you at risk?

If you feel unsafe or uncomfortable, move to a well-lit area, wait somewhere sheltered (your car, a neighbour's house, or a nearby café if open), and call someone you trust to let them know what's happening. Your safety always comes first.

6

Call a Professional Locksmith

If you've checked everything and still can't get in, it's time to call a professional. A qualified locksmith can gain entry without damaging your door, assess whether your lock is faulty, replace or repair the lock if needed, and get you back inside quickly and safely.

Why this is always the best option

Trying to fix the problem yourself often leads to more damage — which costs more in the long run. A locksmith has the right tools and experience to handle the situation properly, usually faster than you'd expect.

7

Ask the Right Questions When You Call

When calling a locksmith, it's worth asking a few quick questions to make sure you know what to expect:

  • How quickly can you get here?
  • Will the entry be non-destructive where possible?
  • What are the expected costs upfront?

A trustworthy locksmith will be transparent and explain the process clearly before any work begins.

8

Once You're Back Inside — Check Your Lock

Getting back inside is the immediate priority, but don't stop there. Take a moment to ask why you got locked out. Is your lock faulty or worn? Could this happen again? Common causes include worn or stiff locks, misaligned doors, damaged keys, and faulty mechanisms. If your lock contributed to the issue, it may be time for a repair or replacement before it happens again.

9

Take Preventative Steps for the Future

Once everything is sorted, it's a great opportunity to make sure it doesn't happen again. Even small changes can save you from another stressful situation down the line.

Keep a spare key with someone you trust
Use a secure key safe by your door
Check for your keys before leaving
Replace worn or unreliable locks
Consider upgrading to a more modern lock
Have your lock inspected if it's been sticking

"The worst thing you can do is panic and force the lock. Most of the time, there's a simple solution — you just need the right approach."

— Sean, DML Locksmiths Ipswich

Getting locked out of your home is never convenient — but it doesn't have to be a disaster. By staying calm, avoiding risky DIY fixes, and calling a professional when needed, you can handle the situation quickly and safely.
